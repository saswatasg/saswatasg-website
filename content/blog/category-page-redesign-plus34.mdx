<AnswerBox>
Category pages failed at Sierra because they treated motive like interchangeable. We replaced commodity grids with story-driven journeys in a 4-week sprint, backed by GA4 custom events and Clarity heatmaps. Result: session-to-PDP click +17%, qualified leads +34%, bounce −16%, ATC +27%, all on 1.1M BigQuery events.
</AnswerBox>

Category pages look simple, but they're the place where buyers decide *"am I in the right store?"* Most stores treat them as static shelves. We treated them as a two-step conversation: **who is this visitor, and which path gets them to a product?**

## Step 1 — Instrument the funnel we didn't have

- **Custom GA4 events** on session start, category view, PDP click, ATC, form start.
- **Clarity heatmaps + scroll depth** across every category template.

The quiet insight: category clicks were big, but *PDP engagement* after the click was weak. Visitors were "buying" a grid, then abandoning floor noise. The top-of-funnel flow was fine; the storytelling was the leak.

## Step 2 — Replace commodity with story

Each of the redesigned category modules balanced:

1. **A guiding first section** — why to choose this category was the layout, not a paragraph
2. **Trust signals up top** — guarantees, shipping, and live rating near the first glance
3. **Intent-filter scaffolding** — the user narrows by aspiration ("feed a family of 6") not by schema variables
4. **Editorial PDP traction** — richer previews; the grid became teasers for the product story

## Step 3 — Measure like an experiment

We didn't launch "the new design." Each module shipped with a controlled rollout and week-over-week comparisons.

## The numbers

| Metric | Δ |
| --- | --- |
| Session→PDP click | **+17%** |
| Qualified leads | **+34%** |
| Bounce rate | **−16%** |
| Add-to-cart rate | **+27%** |
| Events analyzed | 1.1M (BigQuery) |

## What I'd do differently

- **Ship the editorial layer first.** The story section did more than the grid styling; I'd have inverted the order I built.
- **Bank more session-to-PDP variants.** We had one strong winner; a second iteration would've run sooner.
