Soft SaaS features die by neglect. **Send Greetings in LiveKeeping PRO+** was exactly that: technically present, practically unused. The teams kept shipping heavy, but the light portal was dark.

## Why it sat unused

- The feature existed for years but had no reason to be opened.
- Every greeting-launched list was static; no "when."
- Nothing surprised the user into *trying* the generator.
- The templates looked like every other business's spam — same Diwali image, same generic "Happy Diwali" text. No brand identity, no personalization.

## The AI reinvention: model + calendar + timing

We integrated **Google Gemini 3.1 Flash Image (Nano Banana)** so users could build a festival card instantly — no design skills, no assets, no excuses.

But the model alone doesn't fix engagement. The *timing inventory* did:

<Figure src="/blog-assets/ai-send-greetings/calendar.svg" alt="Geo-segmented festival calendar: 27 occasions across 5 Indian regions" caption="27 occasions × 5 regions. The right festival, the right region, the right moment." />

- **A geo-segmented festival calendar** across five Indian regions — Pan-India, South India, Maharashtra/Goa, East India, and Gujarat.
- **27 occasions** — Diwali, Durga Puja, Ganesh Chaturthi, Bihu, Vishwakarma Puja, Gudi Padwa, Ratha Yatra, Onam, Pongal, and 18 more — each assigned the right messaging, language, and visual template.
- **Triggers placed where users already worked** — send-time reminders, invoice completions, payment confirmations, GST filing completions.

## The three-part build

| Part | What it does | Why it moved the needle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AI Greeting Generator** | Select occasion → business name + customer name (pre-filled from ledger) → language → Nano Banana generates a custom card in 15 seconds. No design skills. | The AI was the paint. The user never touched a design tool. |
| **Geo-Segmented Festival Calendar** | 27 occasions Mar–Sep 2026 across 5 regions. A West Bengal trader sees Ratha Yatra and Vishwakarma Puja. A Maharashtra shopkeeper sees Ganesh Chaturthi and Gudi Padwa. | No national blast. The right festival reaches the right user. |
| **Evergreen Greeting Library** | 13 on-demand types any day — Good Morning, Good Night, Thank You (post-payment + post-order), Motivation (Monday + general), Payment Reminder (3 urgency tiers), Sale Promotion, 4 discount tiers. | Usage didn't wait for a festival. Daily habits = daily engagement. |

## The model: Nano Banana (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image)

We chose Nano Banana for three reasons:

1. **Precise text rendering on images** — Indian scripts (Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Gujarati, Telugu) render cleanly on greeting cards. Most open models garble non-Latin scripts.
2. **Multi-language / regional script support** — The model understands "शुभ दीपावली" and renders it correctly in the card, not as tofu boxes.
3. **Greeting card & marketing asset generation** — It was trained for this exact use case: marketing mockups, greeting cards, branded content with in-image text translation.

The integration: a thin wrapper around the Gemini API, cached responses for repeated occasion+language combos, fallback to a pre-rendered template if the API fails. Total integration: ~400 lines of TypeScript.

Each occasion has: a default prompt template (business name, customer name, occasion, language), a visual style guide (color palette, motif), and a trigger date range (e.g., "Diwali: 3 days before through day of").

## The triggers: where the user already is

We didn't build a "greetings tab" and hope they'd visit. We injected the generator into existing workflows:

- **Invoice completion** → "Wish your customer a happy [upcoming festival]?"
- **GST filing success** → "Your return is filed. Wish your clients a prosperous [festival]?"
- **Payment received** → "Thank your customer with a [festival] card?"
- **Send-time reminder** — 9 AM on festival morning: "It's [festival] today. Send wishes?"

The greeting door was the notification banner — not a separate tab.

## The A/B tests that shaped the product

We didn't ship the final version on day one. We tested:

| Test | Variant A | Variant B | Winner | Lift |
|------|-----------|-----------|--------|------|
| Greeting door | Separate tab | Notification banner | Banner | +340% CTR |
| Prompt style | Free text | Occasion picker + prefill | Picker | +220% completion |
| Image model | Stable Diffusion | Nano Banana | Nano Banana | +180% quality rating |
| Timing | Morning only | Morning + invoice | Both | +45% daily active |

The notification banner was the single biggest win. Users don't navigate to features — features must navigate to users.

## The prompt engineering (what actually worked)

The prompt template that produced the best cards:

```
Create a festive greeting card for {occasion}.
Business: {business_name} ({business_type})
Customer: {customer_name}
Language: {language}
Style: {style_guide}
Constraints:
- Include business name prominently
- Include customer name
- Render greeting text in {language} script correctly
- Color palette: {color_palette}
- Aspect ratio: 4:5 (portrait card)
- No watermarks, no extra text
```

We cached responses by `(occasion, language, style_guide)` — 80% of requests hit the cache after week 2. Cache TTL: 7 days.

## The numbers

| Metric | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feature engagement | dormant | **+168%** |
| Occasions surfaced | ~1 national | **27 geo-segmented** |
| Regions covered | 1 | **5** |

Users didn't adopt *an AI generator*. They adopted **a card for the occasion before them** — the AI was the paint, the timing was the purchase.

## What I'd do differently

- **Air the calendar earlier.** The 5-region mapping should've shipped before we raised the model; geo-accuracy was the real feature.
- **Test the greeting door.** Our best surface was a banner inside a richer notification — I'd A/B it against "merchant made a bill → invite to wish a client" earlier.
- **Build the evergreen library first.** The daily-use greetings (Good Morning, Thank You, Payment Reminder) drove 60% of post-launch volume. Festival cards are seasonal; evergreen is the retention engine.
