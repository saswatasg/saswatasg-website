<AnswerBox>
Discovery isn't call stacking; it's a ranked deliberation. At Upcore we ran 20+ prospect interviews across 12 verticals, distilled them into an opportunity brief, and surfaced the top-3 agentic-AI use cases by severity, ROI, and buildability. The scoring frame is the deliverable, not the call logs.
</AnswerBox>

Twenty discovery calls will give you twenty compelling stories, and a roadmap can only hold three. That gap — between what you heard and what you're allowed to build — is where most discovery quietly turns into a highlight reel of the loudest prospect. The fix isn't more calls. It's a decision stack ruthless enough that any executive can trace why use case #1 beat use case #7.

## The interview phase

- **20+ structured calls** with would-be buyers across 12 verticals (manufacturing, logistics, fintech, healthtech, SaaS, edtech, retail, real estate, professional services, media, energy, public sector).
- Each tracked: pain description, budget signal, who "wins" the decision, priority phrasing.
- Interview notes were **signal-rich** — each one landed as a claim, not a story. A note looks like: "VP Ops: manual dispatch causes 3-hr daily loss; budget approved for Q3; CTO owns; phrasing 'we need eyes on every truck'."

## The interview script we actually used

We didn't wing it. Every call followed the same 45-minute structure:

### 1. Context (5 min)
"What does your week look like? Walk me through the last time [problem area] came up."

### 2. Pain mapping (15 min)
- "Describe the last time this hurt. What did you do? What did it cost?"
- "Who else feels this? How many people? How often?"
- "What have you tried? What worked? What didn't?"
- "If this vanished tomorrow, what changes?"

### 3. Budget & authority (10 min)
- "Is there budget allocated for this? Fiscal year or discretionary?"
- "Who signs? Who kills it?"
- "What's the trigger to buy — renewal, incident, mandate?"

### 4. Priority phrasing (10 min)
- "How do you describe this internally? What words?"
- "What's the competing priority that pushes this down?"
- "Show me the roadmap item / Jira epic / Notion page if it exists."

### 5. The magic question (5 min)
"If we built exactly what you described, what would make you *not* buy it?"

That last one surfaced more kill criteria than any feature list. It's the same instinct behind [The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/): people will happily bless your idea; they'll only tell the truth about their constraints.

## Synthesis out of interviews

The synthesis wasn't thematic clustering — it was a scoring machine:

- **Severity** — how sore is this pain? (1–5, anchored to "would they stop work to fix it")
- **ROI** — "would they pay to make it go away?" (budget signal × frequency × time loss)
- **Buildability** — can we ship this without rebuilding the factory? (tech feasibility × data readiness × go-to-market fit)

Every opportunity got a triple score. The **top-3 use cases** by this triple are what a roadmap should be allowed to defend. Everything else is a tasteful "not yet."

### The scoring rubric (what we actually used)

| Score | Severity | ROI | Buildability |
|-------|----------|-----|--------------|
| 5 | Stops work daily; regulatory risk | Budget allocated, urgent | Core competency, data ready, GTM clear |
| 4 | Weekly blocker; workarounds expensive | Budget signaled, timeline defined | Adjacent to core, data accessible |
| 3 | Monthly annoyance; workarounds exist | Budget possible next cycle | New domain, data needs work |
| 2 | Quarterly friction; tolerable | No budget, maybe later | Significant R&D needed |
| 1 | Nice to have; no urgency | No budget, no owner | Research project |

We scored independently (PM + eng lead), then reconciled. Disagreements usually meant "we don't understand the build" — which is a signal itself.

## The reconciliation meeting (where the real decisions happen)

After scoring independently, PM and eng lead sit down with the spreadsheet. The rules:

1. **If scores differ by ≥2 on any dimension, we discuss.** Usually means one of us missed a constraint.
2. **If Buildability ≤ 2, the opportunity dies.** No "we'll figure it out later."
3. **If Severity ≤ 2, it's a vitamin, not a painkiller.** Deprioritize.
4. **The tie-breaker is learning velocity.** Which 0→1 teaches us the most about the market?

This meeting is where the roadmap actually gets written. The scoring is just the prep.

## What the brief said

- **20+ interviews** produced a target pulse: agentic AI for scheduling, orders, and dispatch — the cross-vertical pattern.
- **12 verticals** surfaced cross-functional flow, not a shadow play. The same agentic skeleton applied to manufacturing dispatch, logistics routing, and SaaS onboarding.
- **Top-3 opportunity brief** survived C-suite review, and both a pricing play and an intel play leaned on it.

### The top-3 that survived

| Rank | Use case | Verticals | Severity | ROI | Buildability | Composite |
|------|----------|-----------|----------|-----|--------------|-----------|
| 1 | Agentic dispatch (manufacturing/logistics) | Mfg, Logistics, Wholesale | 5 | 5 | 4 | 14 |
| 2 | AI routing & scheduling (logistics/field services) | Logistics, Field Svcs, Utilities | 4 | 5 | 4 | 13 |
| 3 | Agentic onboarding (SaaS/B2B) | SaaS, Fintech, Edtech | 4 | 4 | 5 | 13 |

The tie at 13 was broken by learning velocity: dispatch had the clearest 0→1 path.

### The pricing play that leaned on the brief

The discovery brief wasn't just a roadmap artifact — it became the pricing framework. For each top-3 use case, we modeled:

| Use case | Pricing metric | Willingness to pay (median) | Model |
|----------|----------------|------------------------------|-------|
| Agentic dispatch | \$/truck/month | \$180 | Per-asset SaaS |
| AI routing | \$/route optimized | \$0.45 | Usage-based |
| Agentic onboarding | \$/seat/month | \$45 | Seat-based |

These numbers came *directly* from the budget signals in discovery. We didn't guess — we asked "what would you pay?" and the median answer became the anchor.

## The slides that survived

- One slide: pain, outcome, budget signal, buildability — for each of the top 3.
- One ranking: "what we'd do 0–6 months" — sequenced by learning velocity, not excitement.
- No "insights" deck with a hundred bullet points. The brief fit in a 6-page memo.

## What I'd do differently

- **Run quant after qual.** Interviews raised questions; a scorecard survey would verify them at 5× reach.
- **Design the interview with scoring baked in.** Asking "how bad is this, 1–10" per pain *during* the call beats post-hoc coding.
- **Version the scoring rubric.** The severity/ROI/buildability weights shifted mid-project; version the rubric so the brief is reproducible.
- **Record the "no" conversations.** The prospects who said "not interested" had the clearest signals about what *would* work.

**Discovery's deliverable isn't what you heard. It's a ranking any skeptic can audit.**
