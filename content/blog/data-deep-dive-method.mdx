<AnswerBox>
A deep dive that changes metrics has two phases. First, instrument the funnel until you can name each drop-off. Second, form ranked hypotheses. At Sierra, 480K sessions became three fixable problems in two weeks — because we never diagnosed an aggregate, only a funnel.
</AnswerBox>

"Cart abandonment is too high." That sentence sat in our metrics review for months, and everyone nodded, and nothing moved — because an aggregate number is unanswerable. When we finally stepped through 480K sessions properly, "abandonment" turned out to be three completely different problems wearing one trench coat. The failure happens four steps before the diagnosis: **no events, no recordings, no funnel, no ranked hypotheses** — and then a hunch gets a roadmap slot.

## Phase 1 — Instrument the funnel

You cannot fix a funnel you cannot step through. The deep dive starts with one ask: *name every step.*

- Install step-level events on the surface you care about (view → engage → convert or checkout view → shipping → payment → purchase)
- Layer on session recordings: heatmaps, rage clicks, scroll depth
- Put the output in BigQuery so you can interrogate it, not just chart it

Only when you can answer "which step loses the most, and which looks like a single cause" do conclusions become credible.

### The GA4 event spec we used at Sierra

```javascript
// Checkout funnel events
dataLayer.push({ event: 'checkout_start', step: 'cart', step_index: 0, value: cartTotal });
dataLayer.push({ event: 'checkout_step', step: 'shipping', step_index: 1, option: shippingMethod });
dataLayer.push({ event: 'checkout_step', step: 'payment', step_index: 2, option: paymentMethod });
dataLayer.push({ event: 'checkout_step', step: 'review', step_index: 3 });
dataLayer.push({ event: 'purchase', value: orderTotal, currency: 'USD', transaction_id: orderId });
```

Every step gets an event. No "checkout started" aggregate — you need the staircase.

### The Clarity setup

[Microsoft Clarity](https://clarity.microsoft.com/) is free and unsampled, which matters more than any premium feature:

- **Recordings on** for 100% of sessions (sampling kills the rare but critical paths)
- **Heatmaps** on every checkout step URL
- **Rage-click detection** on form fields, buttons, promo inputs
- **Dead-click mapping** — clicks on non-interactive elements reveal broken expectations

### The BigQuery schema

We didn't just chart in GA4. The raw events stream to BigQuery via the [GA4 BigQuery export](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9358801), where we can:

```sql
-- Drop-off by step, segmented by device
SELECT
  step,
  device.category,
  COUNT(DISTINCT user_pseudo_id) as users,
  COUNT(*) as events,
  COUNT(DISTINCT user_pseudo_id) / LAG(COUNT(DISTINCT user_pseudo_id)) OVER (ORDER BY step_index) as dropoff_rate
FROM `project.analytics_events`
WHERE event_name IN ('checkout_step', 'purchase')
  AND _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN '20250101' AND '20250131'
GROUP BY step, device.category, step_index
ORDER BY step_index;
```

This SQL is the deep dive. The dashboard is the summary.

## Phase 2 — Ranked hypotheses

Aggregate data lies politely; a funnel stops lying. Take the event tables and rank drop-offs:

| Rank | What you're testing |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The step with the biggest unique loss |
| 2 | The step most likely to be one cause |
| 3 | The step that is easiest to measure after |

<Figure src="/blog-assets/data-deep-dive/funnel.svg" alt="Deep dive method: instrument funnel → rank drop-offs → test hypotheses" caption="Phase 1: instrument. Phase 2: rank. The funnel names the problem; the ranking picks the fix." />

At Sierra this drill surfaced *three distinct* checkout problems — shipping surprise, promo rage-click, payment dead-end — that one aggregate percentage had been hiding.

### How we validated each hypothesis

**Hypothesis 1: Shipping surprise at step 2**
- Evidence: 31% dropoff at shipping step; Clarity rage-clicks on "continue" before shipping loads
- Test: Show total + shipping on cart page
- Measurement: Shipping-step dropoff after 2 weeks

**Hypothesis 2: Promo rage-clicks**
- Evidence: 18% of sessions had ≥3 clicks on promo field; zero submissions with invalid codes
- Test: Inline validation with format hint
- Measurement: Promo-field rage-clicks after 2 weeks

**Hypothesis 3: Payment dead-end**
- Evidence: 9% dropoff at payment; "something went wrong" generic error
- Test: Specific error copy (card type, expiry, network) + retry button
- Measurement: Payment-step recovery rate after 2 weeks

Each hypothesis got a two-week window. If the metric didn't move, the hypothesis was dead.

## The rule that tells this apart from guesswork

- **Two years of aggregate is weaker than two weeks of steps**; instrumentation expands signal, not volume.
- **Cost estimates are hypotheses too.** Just because a fix is one-line doesn't make it the winner.
- **Re-read the funnel after shipping**; you only learn from the same instrument.

## Post-mortem notes

- "Cart abandonment" was the trap; the funnel named three different things.
- Events are facts, recordings are opinions; trust the ones you can replay.
- Full mechanics in [the checkout teardown](/blog/checkout-abandonment-73-to-54).

## What I'd do differently

- I'd start recordings earlier — we had 480K sessions before our first replay window.
- I'd version the funnel definition like source code so "did the fix even change the view" is answerable in a ticket, not a debate.
- I'd build the hypothesis tracker *before* the first event — a simple sheet: hypothesis, owner, KPI, window, result.

## The template you can steal (copy-paste into Notion)

| Hypothesis | Owner | KPI | Window | Evidence needed | Result | Decision |
|------------|-------|-----|--------|-----------------|--------|----------|
| Shipping surprise at step 2 | PM | Shipping-step dropoff | 2 weeks | Clarity rage-clicks ≥30% | Dropoff 31%→12% | Keep |
| Promo rage-clicks | Eng lead | Promo-field rage-clicks | 2 weeks | ≥3 clicks in 18% sessions | Rage-clicks 68%↓ | Keep |
| Payment dead-end | Eng lead | Payment-step recovery | 2 weeks | 9% dropoff, generic error | Recovery 40%↑ | Keep |

Fill this before you write a single event. If you can't fill the "Evidence needed" column, you don't have a hypothesis — you have a hope.

## The anti-patterns that kill deep dives

1. **"Let's just add events and see what happens."** No. Events without a question are noise. Write the hypothesis first.
2. **"We'll A/B test the whole funnel."** You can't attribute. Test one hypothesis per window.
3. **"The dashboard says X."** Dashboards aggregate. The deep dive *disaggregates*. If your dashboard and your deep dive agree, your deep dive failed.
4. **"We don't have time for recordings."** You don't have time *not* to. 50 sessions = 2 hours. The alternative is guessing for months.

**If your dashboard and your deep dive agree, your deep dive failed.** The whole point of disaggregating is to find what the average was hiding.

## Appendix: The form funnel SQL (from the lead-form fix)

```sql
-- Field-by-field drop-off for lead form
SELECT
  field_name,
  COUNT(DISTINCT user_pseudo_id) as reached,
  COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN event_name = 'field_error' THEN user_pseudo_id END) as errors,
  COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN event_name = 'field_submit' THEN user_pseudo_id END) as submitted,
  COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN event_name = 'field_error' THEN user_pseudo_id END) / 
    COUNT(DISTINCT user_pseudo_id) as error_rate
FROM `project.analytics_events`
WHERE event_name IN ('field_focus', 'field_error', 'field_submit')
  AND form_id = 'lead_capture_v2'
  AND _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN '20250101' AND '20250131'
GROUP BY field_name
ORDER BY error_rate DESC;
```

This query revealed the phone field at 23% error rate — double the next worst. The fix was inline validation.

## Appendix: The category page deep dive

For the category page redesign, the funnel was different — session → category view → PDP click → ATC → lead. The key insight came from segmenting by *intent*:

```sql
-- Category-to-PDP by intent signal
SELECT
  category,
  intent_signal,  -- 'browse', 'compare', 'buy_now'
  COUNT(DISTINCT session_id) as sessions,
  COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN event_name = 'pdp_click' THEN session_id END) as pdp_clicks,
  COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN event_name = 'pdp_click' THEN session_id END) / 
    COUNT(DISTINCT session_id) as ctr
FROM `project.analytics_events`
WHERE event_name IN ('category_view', 'pdp_click')
  AND _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN '20250101' AND '20250131'
GROUP BY category, intent_signal
ORDER BY ctr DESC;
```

The "buy_now" intent had 3× the CTR of "browse" — which led to the intent-filter scaffolding in the redesign.
